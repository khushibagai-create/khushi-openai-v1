Show me how an Indian consumer uses ChatGPT.

She is my mother. She is sixty, she lives in Gurgaon, and she runs a kirtan group. She has never made an account. She opens ChatGPT in a browser on her phone and types in Hindi.

Every week there is something to invite people to. She used to ask somebody younger to make the invitation. Now she describes the occasion and gets a finished poster back.

She does not only make posters. In the first screenshot she is setting it a riddle about which of Tinku bhai's three sisters gets the biggest rakhi gift. Nobody taught her that. She does it for fun, which is what a habit looks like before anyone has thought to charge for it.

The rakhi riddle, in Hindi, in a mobile browser, Log in still sitting in the corner. No account, so no memory, no history and no way to charge her.

She has no account for a structural reason, not a personal one. 90% of Android users in India do not use their email IDs. An email-first signup is not friction in Bharat. It is the wall.

I have seen this at scale. Personalised festival and devotional graphics, made to be sent on WhatsApp, is the biggest paid content category in Bharat. It is what Crafto sells and what I spent three years monetising. On a festival day Crafto goes from 3M+ daily actives to 10M.

So the thing my mother is doing on ChatGPT for free is a category that 3M+ Indians already pay for. She is not an edge case. She is the customer, arriving early, through the wrong door.

But eighty percent of Indian messages come from people under 30: students, early-career professionals and developers. A real cohort, and also the one with the least disposable income and the least habit of paying for software in India. My mother, and everybody in her kirtan group, is in the other twenty percent.

One of those made money. What was your part in it?

I was the product manager on conversion, virality and pricing at Crafto from August 2023 to February 2026, which covers that fiscal year. Entrackr attributed the 2.7x jump primarily to higher subscription revenue.

  • D1 conversion up 58% and D7 up 45%, off four changes to onboarding and content format.
  • A two-day free trial lifted overall payment conversion 30%, and 10+ pricing experiments on trial screens improved trial success 38%.
  • Custom placement of a user's photo and name drove +27% on shares and downloads, became 90% of the top ten content in that format, and lifted festival-day payment conversion 21%.

Every experiment behind those numbers is in the resume below.

None of that was a pricing decision. It was a format decision that made the thing worth paying for, and then a price low enough to say yes to. That order is the one I would apply here.

My mother generates the highest-intent commercial content on the platform, several times a month, and OpenAI does not know she exists. There is no upgrade path from a poster, and nothing in the product has ever asked her for one.

Aspiration is universal. The vector it travels through is regional. Why scaling across Bharat becomes tricky

ChatGPT Go went from ₹1,999 to ₹399 to free. That is the cheaper lever, pulled three times, and it has bought enormous reach. The other lever has not been touched, and it is the one that decides what happens when the free year ends.

So what would you actually do about it?

I have not seen your data, so these are the questions I would start from, not a plan.

01

Find the 45-plus cohort in the logs

Not by age, which you do not have, but by behaviour: Hindi and Indic-script prompts, festival and invitation image generation, logged-out mobile web sessions repeating from one device. Size it before arguing about it. My guess is it is larger than anyone has measured, because nobody has looked.

02

Give the output a reason to need an account

The poster is finished, so there is nothing to come back for. Editable saved posters, a reusable style, her group's name remembered between sessions. At Crafto, personalisation was not a feature, it was the entire reason people paid. It is also what an account is for.

03

Price the occasion, not the month

Bharat buys festivals, not subscriptions. Crafto's festival-day payment conversion moved 21% off one format change. When Go stops being free in month thirteen, a flat monthly price converts the under-30s who already pay and loses everyone else.

04

Ship the WhatsApp edge

Everything my mother makes leaves for WhatsApp within seconds and arrives with no idea where it came from. That is a distribution loop running at full volume with attribution off. At Crafto the shareable artefact was the acquisition channel, and two viral experiments lifted shares and downloads 50%.
